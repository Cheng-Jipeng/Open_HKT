## Kahn and Rich(2007): a closer duration analogue for V6 pi

[Kahn and Rich, JME54,1670–1701](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoneco.2006.07.008); [author-hosted published PDF](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jkahn/Papers/kahnrich_jme.pdf).

Table2,p1683 estimates quarterly **high-growth persistence \(q_1=.990\), SE .011**, and low-growth persistence \(p_1=.983\), SE .015. Corresponding mean spells are100 and59 quarters(p1682). The US quarterly sample is1947Q1–2002Q4; an approximate maximum-likelihood Markov dynamic-factor system uses output/hour, compensation/hour, consumption/hour and detrended hours. The per-capita alternative loses regime identification(p1687). The original regimes recur.

**V6 inference:** identify the proposed switch with **first exit** from high productivity growth only as a conditional analogy:
\[
\pi_\Delta=q_1^{4\Delta}=.990^{4\Delta}.
\]
This gives .960596 for one year and .366032 for25years. It is uninterrupted survival, not the original recurrent chain's probability of being high at the endpoint. A high/low aggregate-growth transition still differs from V6's irreversible change from unequal to equal factor spillovers.

**Recommendation:** use this more directly related literature scenario before macro-disaster analogies. Retain low confidence in the structural event mapping. The reported standard error places a conventional Wald upper endpoint above1; it does not deliver a well-behaved confidence interval for duration. Do not call specification differences a credible range. If an analyst duration grid is needed,10/25/50years may be explicitly labeled a stress design, not literature estimates: \(q_D=1-1/(4D)\), \(\pi=q_D^{4\Delta}\). The central25years is source-derived; endpoints are analyst choices.

**Version caution:** earlier2003 drafts use different notation and sample endpoints. Values above are from the published2007 table.

## Bottazzi and Peri(2007): empirical innovation-function specification discipline

[Bottazzi and Peri, Economic Journal117,486–511](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0297.2007.02027.x); [author-hosted published PDF](https://giovanniperi.ucdavis.edu/uploads/5/6/8/2/56826033/bottazzi_et_al-2007-the_economic_journal.pdf).

Their annual15-country OECD panel uses R&D personnel and citation-weighted US patent applications. Dynamic OLS estimates long-run knowledge-stock elasticities to own research and foreign knowledge. Table4,p501 gives \((.786,.557)\) with country effects, versus \((.304,.168)\) with country trends. Text describes1973–1999, while the table note says1972–1999. The knowledge stock depreciates at an imposed10%(p493).

**Exact distinction:** their patent-flow equation(p492) is
\[
Pat=\iota\,RD^\lambda A^\phi A_{ROW}^{\xi},
\]
and the estimated long-run elasticities(p494) are
\[
\ell=\lambda/(1-\phi),\qquad c=\xi/(1-\phi).
\]
V6's innovation law, under otherwise matched definitions, imposes research exponent1, own-knowledge exponent1, no foreign term and no depreciation. That is not a model in which these finite cointegrating ratios identify a portable \(a_i\). Neither source \(\xi\) nor \(\ell\) measures V6's factor-augmentation elasticities.

**Recommendation:** add this source as independent evidence for international spillovers and innovation-law sensitivity, not a numeric prior for \(a_i,\xi_u,\nu_u\). The result strengthens the case for explicit specification checks; no V6 scalar benchmark follows.
