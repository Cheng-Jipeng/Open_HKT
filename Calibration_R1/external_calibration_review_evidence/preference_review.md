# Preference evidence: V6 M01 beta and M02 gamma
Accessed 27 September 2026. Definitions are authoritative from V6_parameter_calibration_groups.tex; supplementary model equations were read in V6_production.tex, lines 544–551. No parameter was changed.

## Exact V6 definitions and mapping rules
\[
U=(1-\beta)\log c_y+\frac{\beta}{1-\gamma}\log E[c_o^{1-\gamma}]
+\text{portfolio terms},\quad
CE_o=(E[c_o^{1-\gamma}])^{1/(1-\gamma)},\quad A_{US}=\beta e_{US}.
\]
M01 beta is a dimensionless old-age utility/saving weight, not an annual discount factor, quasi-hyperbolic present-bias coefficient, or aggregate stock/annual-income ratio. M02 gamma is relative risk aversion over uncertain old-age consumption at unit EIS, not the inverse EIS.

If two-date utility has weights (1,d), beta=d/(1+d). Setting d=delta_annual^Delta additionally assumes an explicit time/cohort bridge; this is our conditional transformation, not an estimate in the papers. For deterministic log annual consumption with stage weights w_y and w_o, beta=w_o/(w_y+w_o), where each weight sums discount factors times any mortality/demographic weights. Equal-length, equally weighted stages with constant consumption can yield the power transformation; unequal stage lengths, pensions, bequests, idiosyncratic risks and nonlog within-stage preferences generally do not.

Gamma has no annualization. A matching consumption-CRRA coefficient maps algebraically unchanged, but its empirical estimate remains conditional on source EIS, labor flexibility, consumption units, population and risks. Converting individual tolerance theta to 1/theta is valid; converting a mean tolerance to a mean gamma is invalid. Heterogeneous-agent aggregation must be specified.

## Evidence ledger

**HKT25 (local pinned v2), beta.** Hirano, Kishi and Toda, *Technological Innovation and Bursting Bubbles*, arXiv:2501.08215v2, Figure 1 note (local source line 602): beta=1/2 is a numerical illustration in the same two-period saving-weight convention. It has high definitional correspondence but supplies no estimated baseline or credible interval, no population sample and no fixed calendar-year interpretation. The local solver's gamma=.5 is a default, not an estimate supplied by that figure. [Version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08215v2).

**GP02, beta and gamma.** Gourinchas–Parker (2002), *Econometrica* 70:47–89, DOI 10.1111/1468-0262.00269. Original Table III p.71 reports annual discount delta=.9598 (corrected SE .0179), CRRA=.5140 (SE .1707), versus delta=.9569 and CRRA=1.3969 under optimal weighting (SE .0150/.1137). Two-stage simulated moments fit US CEX 1980–1993 consumption profiles with PSID income risk; final estimation cell 36,691 households. CRRA utility ties EIS to 1/CRRA, unlike V6. Table V p.79 spans CRRA .1278–5.2823 and delta .8724–.9648; some variants imply implausible initial wealth and are rejected by the authors (pp.78–79). Preserve this specification dependence, not a statistical confidence interval. [Original published PDF, institutional teaching mirror](https://jggarita.github.io/MacroIntermedia/materials/Readings/GourinchasParker2002.pdf).

**LLMRT26, beta and gamma.** Laibson, Lee, Maxted, Repetto and Tobacman, *RFS* 39(6):1580–1610, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhae035 (online 2024, issue 2026). Published Table 3: exponential annual delta=.9601 (SE .0053), CRRA=1.4663 (.2256); present-biased b=.5305 (.1143), delta=.9891 (.0051), CRRA=1.9355 (.4350). MSM fits 16 balance-sheet/age moments using US SCF 1995–2013 credit-card holders with high-school but no college degree. Annual lifecycle environment includes liquidity, retirement, mortality and bequests. The source b is **not V6 beta**. Its .9891 is a continuation discount factor within a present-biased model, not a clean alternative exponential estimate. Source CRRA lacks EZ separation; present bias and liquidity constraints additionally affect effective EIS (footnote39). Earlier working-paper .50/.99/1.3 figures are superseded here. [Published original, Table 3 and Sections 1–4](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/39/6/1580/7703274?rss=1).

**BJKS97, gamma.** Barsky–Juster–Kimball–Shapiro, *QJE*112(2):537–579, DOI10.1162/003355397555280. HRS hypothetical lifetime-income job gambles, older US households and spouses; parametric distribution with response error. Published Table XI pp.562–563: mean RRA12.1 versus harmonic mean4.2; mean tolerance approximately .24 (Table IIA .2412), not the rounded .25 in older abstracts. No representative V6 gamma follows automatically. Lifetime-income risk and hypothetical choice differ from old-age portfolio consumption. [Author-hosted published original](https://websites.umich.edu/~mkimball/keio/5.%20papers/qje-bjks-risk-aversion.pdf).

**KSS08, gamma.** Kimball–Sahm–Shapiro, *JASA*103(483):1028–1038, DOI10.1198/016214508000000139. Equations 1–2 define CRRA over lifetime income W via tolerance theta. HRS began with ages51–61 and spouses; repeated 1992–2002 responses allow likelihood correction for noise and status-quo framing. Final Table4: RRA mean8.2, median6.3, IQR3.9–10.3, 5th–95th percentiles1.9–20.8. These are population heterogeneity percentiles, not confidence bounds for a representative agent. Mean tolerance=.206 implies reciprocal4.9, explicitly different from mean RRA8.2. Mapping to V6 is qualified by age, risk object and aggregation. [Final author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2856097/).

**HL02, gamma.** Holt–Laury, *AER*92(5):1644–1655, DOI10.1257/000282802762024700. Lottery-switch intervals use u(x)=x^(1-r)/(1-r) over experimental cash. Table3 p.1649 contains r=.15–.41, .41–.68, .68–.97 and .97–1.37 categories; these are preference bins, not confidence intervals. Discussion p.1649 centers low-payoff choices around .3–.5; actual larger stakes raise risk aversion. University subjects include undergraduates, MBA students and faculty (p.1647), one-shot choices rather than annual consumption. The paper ultimately rejects constant RRA across payoff scales. Therefore .5 is a weak low-stakes scenario, not strong support for V6 gamma<1. [Original published article scan](https://lorko.sk/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/HoltLaury2002.pdf).

**VA03, gamma.** Vissing-Jørgensen–Attanasio, *AER P&P*93(2):383–391, DOI10.1257/000282803321947399. Numeric locator is the authors' January10,2003 extended manuscript Table2A p.14 (not a claimed journal-page locator): Case4 all-stockholder estimates8.1/6.3/5.5 as human-wealth share rises1/3,2/3,.9; richest-third counterparts28.4/19.9/16.5. For all stockholders at2/3, reported bootstrap95% interval[.3,13.9] excludes uncertainty in some maintained quantities. Semiannual CEX1982–1996 consumption plus asset-return VAR moments, Epstein–Zin preferences and estimated EIS. Directly comparable curvature concept; weak numerical portability because of human-capital, return-tax, aggregation and EIS assumptions. [Author-uploaded full manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4981014_Stock-Market_Participation_Intertemporal_Substitution_and_Risk-Aversion); [published metadata](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/000282803321947399).

**EHI25, gamma (synthesis, not another primary estimate).** Elminejad–Havranek–Irsova, *Journal of Economic Surveys*39(5):2315–2333, DOI10.1111/joes.12689. A meta-analysis of1021 estimates from92 Euler-equation studies separating EIS and RRA; search ended16May2022. Publication-bias corrections yield around1 in economics and2–7 in finance (method-dependent). Table6: US5.20 [2.28,8.11]; overall best-practice3.74 [.99,6.50]. Different contexts and wide model uncertainty preclude pooling these into one V6 estimate. [Published original](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/joes.12689).

## Recommendations, separate from evidence
| V6 parameter | Baseline candidate | Robustness/scenario range | Mapping confidence and justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| beta | No empirically supported V6 point before cohort mapping; .5 may be retained solely as the HKT comparison illustration. | Follow the explicit transformation curve. Illustratively, delta=.9569–.9891 and **assumed**, not estimated, Delta20–40 years yield beta≈.1465–.4454; Delta30 yields .2105–.4185. Include .5 as the separate HKT comparison. These are conditional scenarios, not a literature credible interval. | Low numerical / high algebraic. GP02, LLMRT26 and HKT25. Better to infer the weight after demographic/stage aggregation than import a familiar annual discount. |
| gamma | 5 as a financial-risk candidate; 2 is a separate lifecycle-oriented alternative. Neither is a V6 estimate. | Core scenarios .5,2,5,6.3,10; extend to20 and source-specific higher values. Near1 assess limits or solver handling explicitly; do not silently omit that evidence because displayed V6 utility excludes1. | Medium conceptual / low numerical: VA03, KSS08, EHI25; GP02 and HL02 preserve lower values. No trimming at gamma<1. |

The .5–10 core interval is a proposed sensitivity envelope, not a pooled confidence/credible interval; KSS heterogeneity and VA uncertainty extend beyond it. V6's sufficient bubble theorem requiring gamma<1 is an outcome restriction that may fail under the recommended financial-risk candidates. Report that conflict as economic evidence about the mechanism, rather than relabeling low gamma as a consensus external calibration. Gamma=1's log limit changes the theoretical case; handling it is not completed by this review.
